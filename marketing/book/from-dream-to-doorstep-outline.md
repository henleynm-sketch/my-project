## PART 1: Planning and Vision

### Chapter 1: Should I Build New or Renovate?

**Core Thesis:** It comes down to the bones of the existing structure.

#### Structural Integrity First
- Foundation condition is critical
- Many block foundations are not worth keeping
- Low ceiling heights can be a red flag
- Signs of water issues often indicate deeper problems
- Some older poured foundations are excellent and worth integrating

#### When Renovation Makes Sense
- Good foundation
- Solid framing and exterior walls
- Roof in good shape
- Strong location value
- Ability to add on efficiently

#### When Rebuilding Makes More Sense
- When keeping the structure creates unnecessary cost
- When most components must be replaced anyway
- When you're spending time and money working around something outdated
- When the "value" being preserved isn't actually valuable

#### Emotional & Financial Traps
- Sentimental attachment to something that no longer functions
- Sinking money into small upgrades every year
- "Death by a thousand renovations"
- Underestimating the cost of working around old layouts
- Overestimating what can be reused
- Assuming renovation is always cheaper
- Wanting to keep something because it feels wrong to remove it

#### Smart Alternative Strategy
- Keep existing cottage as guest house/bunkie
- Build new structure if lot size allows
- Integrate strong foundation walls into new design if feasible

### Chapter 2: Common Mistakes Before Talking to a Builder

**Core Mistake:** Assuming options before getting professional input.

- People rely on friends or what they've seen online
- Zoning and municipal allowances change constantly
- Construction methods evolve
- Budget realism is often missing
- Non-professional opinions create confusion
- Even professional opinions vary — so vet your builder carefully

**Advice:** Engage a professional early. Evaluate:
- Reputation
- Volume of projects completed
- Operational structure
- Process clarity
- Recommendations

---

### Chapter 3: How to Choose the Right Location, Lot, or Existing Home

**Two Common Scenarios:**

#### 1. Client Already Owns Property
- Often generational cottages
- Focus shifts to siting
- Evaluate setbacks
- Consider repositioning for better site plan
- Use grandfathered setbacks if possible

#### 2. Client Buying Property
- Builder can assist in selection
- Strong realtor partnerships matter

#### Cottage Country Considerations
- Lake characteristics (swimming depth, shoreline quality)
- Pros and cons of specific Ontario lakes
- Dock usability
- Topography

#### City Build Considerations
- Access to sanitary sewer
- Domestic water availability
- Hydro access
- Septic and well costs if not municipal
- Infrastructure impact on budget

**Final filter:** What works best for the family.

### Chapter 4: When to Bring in a Builder, Designer, or Architect

**Ideal Timing:** Early. As early as possible.

#### Balanced Approach
- Begin professional conversations early
- Simultaneously clarify:
  - Family goals
  - Budget reality
  - Financing options

#### Important
- Builders need financial clarity upfront
- Budgets vary in flexibility
- Early connection allows:
  - Financing referrals
  - Clear expectations
  - Strategic direction

### Chapter 5: What Budget Do You Actually Need?

#### Major Themes

1. **Scope & Finishes Drive Cost** — Realistic estimating is critical
2. **Contingency Is Essential** — Do not stretch to your financial maximum. Leave margin. Maintain flexibility.
3. **Budget Communication Matters** — Some clients are flexible, some are strict. Builder must know which.
4. **Stretching to Max Requires Clarity** — Must be communicated early. Changes and upgrades become harder at capacity.
5. **Must-Haves vs Nice-to-Haves** — Prioritize intelligently.

**Philosophy:** No one wants to be forced into cutting good ideas because they were already maxed out.

---

### Chapter 6: Protecting Yourself From Unexpected Costs

**Core Principle:** It's about knowing the right questions to ask.

#### Protection Strategies
- Work with someone transparent
- Review FAQs and documentation together
- Develop trust
- Builder adjusts to homeowner goals
- Flexibility matters because every project is different

**Key Message:** Homeowners shouldn't be forced into a builder's rigid system. The builder should adapt to client goals.

## PART 2: Design & Pre-Construction

### Chapter 7: How to Get From Idea to Buildable Plan

#### Process Approach

1. **Collaborate with Builder Early** — Design must match budget and be practical
2. **Inspiration Process** — Shared Pinterest boards, categorized by:
   - Kitchen
   - Bathroom
   - Exterior
   - General style
   - Visual alignment early

This prevents:
- Misalignment
- Unrealistic expectations
- Cost surprises later

---

### Chapter 8: What Is a Feasibility Study?

**Your Process:** Conduct feasibility before offering a design proposal.

#### Key Points
- High-level viability assessment
- Budget alignment check
- Site constraints review
- Zoning considerations
- Early risk identification

#### Strategic Advantage
- Do not recommend design phase unless project is feasible
- Feasibility summary included at top of proposal
- Clear statement: "You qualify for this process"

This builds trust and filters poor-fit projects early.
